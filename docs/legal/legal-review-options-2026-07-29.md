## 2. Detail on each option

### 1. qLegal (Queen Mary University of London) — £0

**Eligibility is clear and RapidGlobe probably meets it.** Published criteria: _"You are a profit seeking/making organisation whose annual turnover is under £100,000; or You are a social enterprise/not-for-profit whose annual turnover is under £1,000,000."_ Crucially, qLegal **explicitly admits for-profit companies** — unlike almost every other free scheme.

Covered areas include _"complying with your responsibilities to keep data secure"_ and _"documenting the key terms to protect your commercial position"_ (NDAs, T&Cs) — a direct match for both documents. Also covers technology/online operations including AI.

**Constraints:**

- **Currently closed for new enquiries; reopens July/August 2026** — i.e. right now. Apply as soon as the form is live.
- Cannot assist with _"matters involving disputes"_ (not relevant here).
- Advice is delivered by postgraduate law students supervised by qualified lawyers. That is not the same as a solicitor's professional sign-off, and there is no professional indemnity comfort of the kind a regulated firm gives.
- Document review is not advertised as a service — the model is an advice session. Expect a discussion of gaps, not tracked changes.
- Strict attendance rules: cases are closed for missing appointments, arriving 10+ minutes late, rescheduling twice, or failing to give 72 hours' notice.

**Verdict:** best free option by a distance. Turnover under £100k is the gating question — check RapidGlobe's last filed accounts before applying.
Source: https://www.qmul.ac.uk/qlegal/clients/legaladvice/

### 2. University of Law — Small Business Advice Line — £0

Free **30-minute appointment with a commercial solicitor**, delivered virtually on rota clinic days, daytime or evening. Listed topics: _"Contract terms • Privacy • Shareholder entitlement • Data protection • Setting up a company"_. Multiple campuses run it (Sheffield: sbal@law.ac.uk; Birmingham and London equivalents exist). The University of Law's own page describes a _"free 30–45-minute telephone advice appointment with a lawyer"_ covering _"privacy, trademark, contracts, business structures"_.

**Constraints:**

- **"Initial advice only"** and **"No written advice is given to the clients"** — this is the significant limitation. You cannot obtain a written sign-off, which is what a pre-launch audit trail usually wants.
- Cannot undertake casework.
- The University of Law's public page frames eligibility as people _"not eligible for legal aid and cannot afford to pay"_ — a means-flavoured test that is soft but real.
- 30 minutes is not enough to review two documents properly. Use it to test 3–4 specific questions.

**Verdict:** free, fast, and worth doing regardless — but as a sanity check, not a sign-off.
Sources: https://www.lawworks.org.uk/legal-advice/individuals/university-law-sheffield-small-business-advice-line ; https://www.law.ac.uk/about/legal-advice-for-the-public/

### 3. Lawhive — c. £100–£200 per document

SRA-regulated (**Lawhive Legal Ltd, SRA ID 8003766**), which matters: you get a regulated solicitor, professional indemnity cover and access to the SRA compensation fund. Free initial consultation to scope the matter before paying.

The only figure Lawhive publishes for this kind of work is on the website terms of use page: _"On average it is expected to range from £100-£150, but in some cases, it could cost as much as £200."_ The privacy policy page and GDPR compliance page publish **no figures at all** — both say "transparent pricing" and route you to a quote form. A third-party April 2026 comparison cites _"from £149 for a GDPR policy"_ — treat that as indicative only, not as Lawhive's published price.

**Constraints:**

- Prices quoted are framed around **drafting**; confirm explicitly that reviewing an existing draft is priced the same or lower.
- Two documents likely means two matters, so budget roughly £200–£400 total.
- Confirm in writing whether a follow-up round (re-reviewing your amendments) is included — this is the single most common hidden cost.

**Verdict:** cheapest genuinely regulated route. Strong candidate.
Sources: https://lawhive.co.uk/services/website-terms-of-use ; https://lawhive.co.uk/small-business/privacy-policy ; https://lawhive.co.uk/services/gdpr-compliance

### 4. LawBite — from £165 + VAT per document review

SRA-regulated online law firm aimed squarely at SMEs. Free 15-minute consultation, then a fixed-price quote. Reported published prices (April 2026): **fixed-fee contract review from £165 + VAT for documents up to 20 pages, ~3 working days**; hourly advice **£180 + VAT**; subscriptions **£49/mo + VAT** (Access) and **£149/mo + VAT** (Growth, bundling 10 hours of advice annually). Lawyers average 10+ years post-qualification experience. ICAEW members get a free consultation and 15% discount — check whether the client's accountant can introduce them.

**Constraints and a caution:**

- **LawBite's own website (lawbite.co.uk / www.lawbite.co.uk) did not resolve when checked on 29 July 2026.** That may be a transient DNS issue on this network, but every price above comes from a third-party April 2026 comparison, not from LawBite. **Verify directly before relying on these figures.**
- "Contract review" is the advertised product; confirm a privacy policy and website terms count as in-scope documents at the £165 tier.
- Two documents = likely 2 × £165 + VAT ≈ £396 inc. VAT.
- Avoid the subscriptions. £149/mo + VAT is £1,788/yr + VAT for a one-off review need — bad value here.

Source: https://nouveaulegal.co.uk/7-best-providers-of-affordable-start-up-legal-advice-in-the-uk-2026/ (article dated April 2026)

### 5. Rocket Lawyer UK — £209.88/yr, document review included

Published: **£34.99/mo charged monthly**, or **£17.49/mo charged annually at £209.88** (crossed-through from £419.88), after a 7-day free trial. Membership includes **document review** and "Ask a Lawyer". Add-on professional services at member rates: legal advice sessions **£180/hour**; business health check **£0** for the first one for Rocket Legal+ members (normally £180); trade mark first registration £0 (normally from £280 excl. IPO fees).

**Constraints and traps:**

- **The pricing page says nothing about SRA regulation.** Do not assume the review is performed by a solicitor of a regulated firm — establish who reviews, and under what regulatory status, before paying.
- The free trial then auto-converts to a paid subscription. If used tactically, cancel deliberately; otherwise this becomes a recurring cost for a one-off need.
- Membership "document review" is typically a light-touch check against a template model, not a bespoke compliance opinion on an AI service processing personal data across four sub-processors. Expect it to catch drafting sloppiness, not architectural data-protection questions.

Source: https://www.rocketlawyer.com/gb/en/pricing

### 6. Singleton Solicitors — £480 + VAT for BOTH documents

This is the **only published fixed fee found that covers the exact job in one price**: £480 to check a website's terms and conditions **and** privacy policy and confirm the site's legal compliance, plus VAT where applicable. Susan Singleton is a solicitor, a Law Society member, and SRA-regulated, with a long-standing specialism in commercial, IT and EU/competition law.

**Constraints:**

- **The singlelaw.com site returned connection errors (ECONNRESET) on every attempt on 29 July 2026**, across http/https and with/without `www`. The £480 figure is quoted from the firm's own "Our Terms & Typical Costs" page as surfaced in search results, and the page carries a 2026 price-guide framing — but I could not open the page to confirm the wording, the date, or whether the fee has moved. **Confirm by email before committing.**
- Sole practitioner: excellent depth, but single-point capacity risk on turnaround.
- Confirm whether a second round (re-reading your amended drafts) is included or billed extra.
- The scope is website compliance. Explicitly flag the AI-specific angles (Anthropic Claude via Amazon Bedrock as a sub-processor, no model training, UK/EEA-only processing) so they are inside scope rather than treated as out of it.

Source: https://www.singlelaw.com/our-terms

### 7. Jonathan Lea Network — £250–£350 + VAT per session, £500 + VAT minimum to open a file

Full-service SRA-regulated firm (founded 2019, Haywards Heath) with a commercial/tech and e-commerce practice, and unusually transparent published fees. **Free 20-minute phone/video call** at the firm's discretion. **Fixed-fee appointments £250–£350 + VAT** depending on complexity and solicitor seniority. Capped fixed fees offered for straightforward matters.

**The trap here is explicit and published:** _"We generally have a minimum fee of £500 plus 20% VAT (£600 inclusive of VAT) to open a new file."_ So a "£250 appointment" can become a £600 engagement once it becomes a matter rather than a call.

**Verdict:** use the free 20-minute call to get a capped fixed-fee quote for both documents. Only proceed if that quote beats Singleton's £480 + VAT.
Source: https://www.jonathanlea.net/our-fees/

### 8. decoded.legal — £600 + VAT per questionnaire review; £1,200–£1,800 + VAT scoping

The most technically appropriate reviewer found for the privacy side. Neil Brown is a solicitor specialising in data protection and privacy, internet/technology law, security and communications regulation. SRA-regulated: **decoded.legal Limited, SRA 626329** — insured, with compensation fund access.

Published prices for the UK GDPR / data protection compliance review, by phase:

- **Phase 1 — initial scoping:** £1,200 + VAT (greater involvement by you) to £1,800 + VAT (lesser involvement by you)

The general pricing page declines to publish a rate card (_"can't give you a menu of prices"_), offering fixed fees, hourly rates, or monthly subscriptions (blocks of five hours, from 20 hours/month, use-it-or-lose-it).

**Verdict:** over budget for the full phased review, but **worth a scoping email**. The Phase 3 "policies and procedures review" priced on sight of the materials could come in materially below the phased figures, since the drafts already exist and the data map is simple and documented. This is the option to pick if the client wants one authoritative privacy opinion rather than two cheap ones.

Source: https://wdps.co.uk/about/privacy-rates/

## 3. Routes examined and ruled out (not counted against the 10)

Being blunt here rather than padding the shortlist:

- **LawWorks Not-for-Profits Programme — NOT ELIGIBLE.** This is the obvious first thought and it is a dead end. The published criteria require the applicant to _be_ a not-for-profit: _"We only provide assistance to not-for-profit organisations"_ — charitable companies, CICs, CIOs, unincorporated associations. A private limited company is excluded **even though it provides a free service to charities**. The scheme serves charities, not their suppliers. Data protection and commercial/contract law _are_ within its covered areas, and the £1m income threshold would be met — but entity type is a hard gate. Search results also indicate the programme has been **temporarily closed to new applications** owing to application volume. Sources: https://www.lawworks.org.uk/legal-advice-not-profits/eligibility-criteria-and-guidance ; https://www.lawworks.org.uk/legal-advice-not-profits/free-legal-assistance
- **TrustLaw (Thomson Reuters Foundation) — ELIGIBILITY DOUBTFUL, and too slow.** Free pro bono matching for NGOs **and social enterprises**, which is the one scheme where a company _might_ squeeze in. But the test is that you _"operate as a non-profit or as a social enterprise (namely more than 50% of your profits need to be reinvested into delivering more impact)"_ and have operated for at least a year. RapidGlobe Ltd is a conventional company limited by shares with no asset lock or reinvestment constraint in its constitution, so it would be arguing eligibility uphill. Matching also takes weeks-to-months and is aimed at high-impact projects — a poor fit for a pre-launch blocker. Free to apply, so no harm in trying in parallel, but do not plan around it. Source: https://vc4a.com/lefil-consulting/trustlaw/
- **The Chancery Lane Project — NOT APPLICABLE.** It is a free library of 250+ climate-aligned model clauses plus training and peer sessions. It does not review anyone's documents and has nothing to do with privacy or website terms. Source: https://chancerylaneproject.org/
- **Advocate — NOT APPLICABLE.** Matches _"members of the public"_ who cannot get legal aid or afford to pay with volunteer **barristers**. Barristers give advocacy and opinions on disputes; this is a company needing transactional document review. Source: https://weareadvocate.org.uk/
- **Sprintlaw UK — EXCLUDED on two grounds.** It publishes **no prices at all** (only a general _"£100 to £1,500"_ range), and its own footer states: _"We are a legal consultancy, not a law firm, and are not authorised or regulated by the Solicitors Regulation Authority."_ Its Startup Privacy and Terms Starter Pack is also **drafting only** — the page offers new documents, not review of existing ones. Note that at least one third-party comparison wrongly lists Sprintlaw as SRA-regulated; the footer is authoritative. Sources: https://sprintlaw.co.uk/startups/startup-privacy-and-terms-starter-pack/ ; https://sprintlaw.co.uk/platform/pricing/
- **DPO-as-a-Service — WRONG PRODUCT, AND AN EXPENSIVE TRAP.** Market rates run **from £695/month** at the cheap end, with typical UK fractional DPO retainers at **£500–£2,500/month** and properly resourced engagements at **£1,500–£5,000/month**. RapidGlobe is a small private company doing low-risk processing and is almost certainly **not under a statutory duty to appoint a DPO** at all. Buying a monthly DPO retainer to solve a one-off document review would be the single worst-value decision available here. If any provider proposes this, treat it as a sales upsell. Sources: https://dpo-service.co.uk/ ; https://www.engagecompliance.co/best-outsourced-dpo-providers-uk-2026 ; https://evalian.co.uk/dpo-as-a-service-costs-a-comprehensive-guide/
- **Catalyst Law (£200 + VAT key points / £350 + VAT detailed report, SRA 634437)** — genuinely cheap and regulated, with a clarification call included in both tiers, but its published list of reviewable documents covers supply/services/employment/loan/construction contracts, business T&Cs, NDAs and consultancy agreements — **website terms and privacy policies are not listed**. Cut for scope mismatch rather than price. Source: https://www.catalystlaw.co.uk/business-contract-review.html
- **PAIL Solicitors** — SRA-regulated (827265) and does exactly this work, but publishes **no figures** ("Fixed-fee proposals provided following consultation", "Fixed Fee Consultation £TBC"). Cut for lack of a real price. Source: https://pailsolicitors.co.uk/website-terms-conditions-lawyers
- **Net Lawman document review** — a third-party snippet indicates a review service at around £250 with ~2 working days' turnaround, but **Net Lawman's own pages do not publish the price** (they direct you to support@netlawman.co.uk). Not counted, because the figure is not sourced to the provider. Source: https://www.netlawman.co.uk/guide-editing-legal-documents
- **FSB membership (£345/yr for 1–10 employees; associate from £97.50/yr; £50 off first year with code FIFTY until 31/07/2026)** — includes a 24/7 legal advice line staffed by UK-qualified lawyers (03450 727 727) and a library of 1,500+ legal templates. Genuinely useful for a small business generally, but the advice line gives **guidance, not document review or sign-off**, and their own hub page could not be read to confirm otherwise. Cut because it does not deliver the deliverable. Source: https://fsbmembership.co.uk/price/ ; https://www.fsb.org.uk/membership/benefits/legal-and-business-hub
- **Business bank legal advice lines** — searched; no current UK business account was found publishing a free legal advice line as an included benefit. Nothing credible here.

## 4. Free authoritative self-help — do this first, whichever provider you pick

None of these count against the 10. Every hour of drafting quality you add here is an hour you do not pay a solicitor to find.

1. **ICO Privacy Notice Generator (free).** Guides you through questions and populates a template covering the elements required by law, with sector-specific paths (including charity and voluntary sector). Produces two notice types: customer/supporter and supplier (for websites), and staff/volunteer (internal). Generate one and **diff it against `docs/legal/privacy-policy.md`** — anything the generator includes that your draft omits is a gap you found for free.
   https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/privacy-notices-and-cookies/create-your-own-privacy-notice/
   Announcement: https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2024/08/new-tool-helps-small-businesses-create-privacy-notices/

2. **ICO data protection self-assessment toolkits (free).** Separate checklists for small business owners/sole traders and for medium-sized organisations. Each generates a short report with a compliance rating, practical actions and links to guidance.
   https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/getting-started-with-gdpr/assessment-for-small-business-owners-and-sole-traders/
   https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/getting-started-with-gdpr/data-protection-self-assessment-medium-businesses/

3. **ICO SME helpline — 0303 123 1113, select option 4 (free).** A dedicated route to ICO staff for sole traders and small businesses. Free, authoritative, and the regulator's own view. Use it for the specific questions: sub-processor disclosure for Amazon Bedrock, transparency wording for AI processing, and retention of uploaded funder guidelines and user-written answers.
   https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/

4. **ICO "Advice for small organisations" hub** — free guidance and checklists across the whole compliance surface; the natural home base for the above.
   https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/

**Point to raise with whoever you engage:** the drafts should be reviewed specifically against the AI processing chain — Anthropic Claude via Amazon Bedrock (eu-west-2), Supabase (London), Vercel (London), Sentry (EU) — as named sub-processors, the "no data used to train models" representation, UK/EEA-only processing, and retention of uploaded funder guideline documents and user-authored answers. A generic website-terms review will not cover these, and they are the parts most likely to be wrong.

## 5. Recommendation

### Top three

**1. Singleton Solicitors — £480 + VAT (c. £576 inc. VAT) — best single-purchase answer.**
The only published fixed fee found that covers **both documents in one price**, from an SRA-regulated solicitor with a genuine commercial/IT specialism. ### Traps to avoid

- **Monthly subscriptions for a one-off need.** LawBite Growth at £149/mo + VAT is £1,788/yr + VAT; DPO-as-a-service starts at £695/mo. Both are wildly wrong for a single review.
- **Assuming a provider is SRA-regulated.** Sprintlaw's own footer says it is not, despite third-party comparisons claiming otherwise. Rocket Lawyer's pricing page is silent on it. Check the SRA register before paying: https://www.sra.org.uk/consumers/register/
- **Minimum file-opening fees.** Jonathan Lea Network publishes a £500 + VAT minimum to open a file — a £250 "appointment" is not the real cost of an engagement.
- **Buying a template and calling it a review.** It is not independent, and it gives no audit trail for "reviewed before launch".
- **Letting the AI/sub-processor questions fall outside scope.** They are the highest-risk part of these documents and the part a generic website-compliance review will skip. Name them in the engagement letter.
- **Free trials that auto-convert.** Rocket Lawyer's 7-day trial rolls into £34.99/mo unless cancelled.
