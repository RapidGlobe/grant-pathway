## 1. Product Vision and Positioning

### 1.1 What Grant Pathway is

Grant Pathway is a **preparation tool** that helps UK charitable organisations write better grant applications. It guides the charity through understanding a funder's requirements, assists with the structure and clarity of the narrative answers the funder asks for, and produces copy-ready output that the charity then submits through the funder's own channel.

**Grant Pathway uses AI to assist the charity in writing — not to generate content on its behalf.** The charity writes every substantive answer. AI helps organise, structure, and clarify what the charity has already said. This distinction matters: the application reflects the charity's authentic voice, specific knowledge, and real community experience — not generic AI output. This is also what funders expect and value (see Section 1.4).

**Grant Pathway is not a submission platform.** It does not submit applications to funders, integrate with funder portals, or manage submission deadlines. These responsibilities remain with the charity.

### 1.2 Core value proposition

Grant writing is time-consuming, repetitive, and often opaque. Charities — particularly small and medium-sized ones — frequently lack the capacity to write compelling, well-structured grant applications for multiple funders simultaneously. Grant Pathway removes the blank-page problem: it extracts what each funder is actually looking for, helps the charity articulate their work in the funder's language, and assembles the charity's own written answers into a structured, reviewable document ready for export. Where a charity clearly isn't eligible for a funder, this is flagged early so no time is wasted on an application that was never going to succeed.

### 1.3 What Grant Pathway is not

- A submission platform or portal integration
- A funder database or grant-finding service
- A document management system for supporting documents
- An automated application generator — Grant Pathway uses AI to assist, not to generate. The charity writes every substantive answer.

### 1.4 AI usage principle — assist, not generate

**Grant Pathway uses AI to assist, not to generate.** This principle is stated explicitly throughout the product — in onboarding, in Step 4 guidance, and in the export disclaimer — and is not negotiable. It is not merely a design preference; it reflects what UK funders consistently require and what produces better application outcomes for charities.

## 2. Target Users

### 2.1 Primary users

**Charity staff responsible for fundraising and grant applications** at small to medium-sized UK registered charities. Typically:

- Income band: £50,000 – £2,000,000 per annum
- Team size: 1–10 people; fundraising often shared across roles
- Technical literacy: moderate — comfortable with web applications, not developers
- Core pain point: the capacity and time to produce well-structured, compelling applications across multiple funders — not lack of knowledge about their own work. They know their organisation deeply; Grant Pathway helps them express it in the language and format each funder requires

### 2.2 Secondary users

**Senior staff and trustees** who review and approve applications before submission. Grant Pathway's senior review prompt and approval step are designed with this role in mind.

Senior involvement is particularly important — and should be treated as mandatory — for any application section covering financial information. The treasurer, finance lead, or a trustee with financial oversight must review and verify all financial figures before the application is submitted. No AI-assisted tool can substitute for the senior financial sign-off that funders expect and that protects the charity's integrity. Grant Pathway surfaces this requirement explicitly at the preparation checklist stage and again at the senior review prompt before assembly.

### 2.3 Who Grant Pathway is not designed for

- Organisations outside the UK charitable sector

## 4. Charity Profile — The Organisation Vault

### 4.1 Purpose

The charity profile is the organisation's permanent data store within Grant Pathway. It serves two functions:

1. **Context for AI** — the profile is passed to the AI on every application to ensure responses reflect the charity's voice, mission, and track record.
2. **Pre-fill source for the profile itself** — the profile's own identity fields (Section 4.2.1) are populated from the Charity Commission register. This does _not_ extend to pre-filling non-narrative _questions on a funder's application form_.

### 4.2 Profile fields

#### 4.2.1 Identity

**Primary data source: Charity Commission for England and Wales (built). Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR) and Charity Commission Northern Ireland (CCNI) — planned, not yet built.**

Read plainly, that stated all three registers were integrated. **Only England and Wales is.** `actions/charity.ts` queries one endpoint — `api.charitycommission.gov.uk` — and there is no reference to OSCR or CCNI anywhere in the codebase. The v0.16 change was made to name the registers "plainly" rather than to claim coverage, but the qualifier it removed was doing necessary work. Found when the wording in the external Business Overview was questioned against this document; the overview was correct and this was not.

The Charity Commission for England and Wales publishes a publicly accessible register of charities registered in England and Wales. Grant Pathway integrates with this register via the charity profile lookup (the charity searches by name or registration number on the profile page). The register provides: registered name, registration number, date of registration, and organisation type. The charity confirms and may edit these values after lookup — the register is the starting point, not the final word.

**UK-wide register coverage — current position and roadmap:**

Grant Pathway is **available to** charities across the whole of the United Kingdom, and every charity has full access to the application workflow regardless of where it is registered. **Automatic register lookup, however, currently covers England and Wales only.** Charities registered in Scotland (OSCR, oscr.org.uk — approximately 24,000 charities) and Northern Ireland (CCNI, charitycommissionni.org.uk — approximately 7,000) **enter their profile details manually in v1.** These are independent registers with their own APIs, separate from the England and Wales Charity Commission, and each needs its own integration.

That integration is `FP-07` in `docs/future-phases.md`, carrying decision record `BD-02` and the status **"planned before general release"** — a pre-launch commitment. It is now tasked as `P5.7` in `IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md`; before 2026-07-31 it was a stated intention with no task anywhere, which is how it reached this point unbuilt. **Nothing user-facing may describe the lookup as UK-wide until `P5.7` is built and tested.**

#### 4.2.3 Financial and governance — moved out of the charity profile (2026-07-15)

These 5 fields (total expenditure, reserves, trustees related, bank signatory count, bank signatories related) were added here 2026-07-05 because two funders' actual guidelines (Walton Charity, MK Community Foundation) required financial and governance facts the profile didn't previously capture. Investigation on 2026-07-15 found they were never actually consumed anywhere as profile fields — not by Step 3's summary/eligibility logic, not by P6.5's application-reuse feature — so this section's original promise was never realised in practice.

**Decision (WJ, 2026-07-15):** re-site these 5 facts into the application flow itself (Step 4), collected fresh per application rather than once per charity — no charity profile field, no cross-application carry-forward, not even via P6.5's "start from your last application" reuse. The charity profile (§4.2.1–4.2.2) reverts to just Identity and Mission and work. See `ADR-DATA-006`'s 2026-07-15 amendment and `data-model.md` §4c for the full technical design; `PRD-Grant-Pathway.md` Screen 7 (Step 4) for the user-facing behaviour.

### 4.3 Profile completeness indicator

Until the charity profile is fully saved, starting a new application is blocked with a tooltip directing the charity to complete their profile first, and a dashboard banner prompts the same. This is a simple complete/incomplete gate — the app does not show which specific fields are missing, and there is no mechanism indicating which funder questions would or would not be pre-filled as a result.

## 5. Application Workflow

### 5.1 Overview

Grant Pathway uses a five-step linear workflow. Step 3 (AI Summary) and Step 4 (Draft Answers) adapt based on what the AI extracts from the guidelines.

```
Step 1: Application Details
Step 2: Upload Guidelines
Step 3: AI Summary
Step 4: Draft Answers
Step 5: Review and Export
```

### 5.2 Step 1 — Application Details

The charity enters:

- Grant name / programme name

The charity selects the funder from a searchable picker of the approved funder directory, or enters a funder name directly if it isn't found in the picker (`DR-FD-001`, amended 2026-07-11 — free-text fallback to be trialled in testing, not yet implemented in code). No coverage tier is displayed anywhere in the app.

### 5.3 Step 2 — Upload Guidelines

The charity uploads the funder's published guidelines document (PDF or Word) or pastes guidelines text directly. This is the source the AI analyses at Step 3.

### 5.4 Step 3 — AI Summary

The AI analyses the uploaded guidelines and produces a structured summary. The summary includes:

| Field                    | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| About this grant         | 2–3 sentences: funder name, programme, purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Grant amount             | Range as stated in guidelines                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Who can apply            | Eligibility criteria as bullet points                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| What they're looking for | Funding priorities as bullet points                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Key requirements         | Important restrictions, exclusions, deadlines                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| Funder AI policy         | Extracted and stored on every summarisation call, but not displayed to the charity -- the Step 3 summary screen's AI-policy banner was deliberately removed (see `docs/Implementation Plan/CHANGELOG.md`); the field remains available in the data model only |
| Application questions    | Narrative questions or sections extracted from the guidelines — discrete numbered questions where the guidelines specify them, or narrative themes/headings with guidance text where they don't. Data-entry and non-text fields are excluded                  |

Whether a guideline produces discrete questions or narrative sections is derived per-application from that application's own AI summary — not a stable property of the funder (`DR-FD-001`).

The charity reviews the summary and confirms it is accurate before proceeding to Step 4.

### 5.5 Step 4 — Draft Answers

#### Question-level typing

Step 4 will handle each item in the funder's form individually, not as one flat list. Alongside its type, an item can also:

- only appear if an earlier answer makes it relevant (so forms that branch — e.g. "if you answered yes to Q3, also complete Q4" — work correctly)
- be marked as either a hard rule Grant Pathway can check automatically (e.g. "must not exceed 80% of total costs") or a judgement call it can only flag as a caution, not enforce (e.g. "clearly demonstrates community need")

| Item type            | How it appears in Step 4                                                                   |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Narrative            | Writing card with textarea, word/character counter, AI assist button (as today)            |
| Data / date / number | Pre-filled read-only field from the charity profile, with an edit option                   |
| Table                | Structured entry matching the funder's own budget/table shape                              |
| File                 | Read-only reminder — "you'll need to attach X" — not silently dropped                      |
| Consent              | Read-only reminder; never profile-pre-filled (sensitive, no-default)                       |
| Eligibility gate     | Drives the mismatch check (FR-47) per-criterion, not one blanket AI judgement              |
| Scoring criterion    | Coaches how to write a stronger narrative answer against the funder's own published rubric |
| Manual action        | Flagged as something the charity must do outside the app (signature, mandatory call)       |

**Amended 2026-07-14 (P6.5):** the "curated funder playbook" concept below — a single, shared, human-reviewed record per funder, approved once and reused by every charity — was designed but never built, and was superseded before build by a simpler, private model: see `ADR-DATA-006`'s 2026-07-14 amendment. The paragraph is left as the historical record of the original intent.

~~Each funder gets a **playbook** — a one-off, curated record of exactly how that funder's form works: its items, budget shape, scoring rubric, and output format (`ADR-DATA-006`). Rapidglobe builds a first draft with AI, then a human checks and corrects it once. Every future application to that funder reuses this same reviewed playbook, rather than the AI re-guessing from scratch each time a charity applies.~~ **What's actually built (P6.5):** when a charity starts a new application for a funder they've already reached Step 4 with before, they're offered an explicit choice — start fresh, or start from their own last application to that funder, carrying across the question list, retained guidelines, AI summary, and their previous answers (marked "needs review"). This is entirely private to that one charity's account — no shared playbook, no curator role, no cross-charity reuse.

Each funder also shows a visible, honest status — fully supported, partially supported (with the specific gaps named), or guidance-only — so the product never quietly overclaims what it can actually do for that funder.

Two things Grant Pathway deliberately won't try to do:

- It will only ever produce a generic Word/text document, never fill in the funder's own template or portal form directly. Correctly filling an arbitrary funder's exact format was judged too hard to do reliably, given how differently funders build their forms — so it's ruled out for good, not just deferred (R9, `docs/v1-out-of-scope.md`).
- If a funder scores something the charity was never actually asked a question about — for example, a score based on the charity's past history applying to that same funder — Grant Pathway will say so plainly rather than guess. The charity sees a clear note that this factor exists, affects their score, and is something Grant Pathway can't assess on their behalf (R16, see Section 10, BD-06 note).

#### Limit handling

Every narrative question carries a `word_limit` or `char_limit` (integer, whichever applies) and a `limit_type` (`words`, `characters`, or `none`) determining which one is relevant. The Step 4 counter displays:

- "120 / 800 characters" for character-limited questions
- "85 / 200 words" for word-limited questions

Limits come from the guidelines. Where limits are not stated, no counter is shown.

#### AI assistance — assist, not generate

The "Help me improve this" button is available on all narrative questions. It is disabled on questions flagged as budget/financial (`is_budget_question`). **It refines structure, flow, and clarity — it does not add, invent, or generate new information.** The charity's words and facts remain the foundation; AI only improves how they are expressed. This means the application continues to reflect the charity's authentic voice — which is what funders assess and value.

**Known limitation:** large language models cannot reliably hit an exact word or character count when compressing an over-limit answer. The charity must check the counter after accepting a suggestion; see `PDR-AI-006` for current messaging behaviour.

Budget/financial questions have no AI assist at all, so this doesn't apply to them; see `PDR-AI-007` for current over-limit messaging behaviour for these questions.

#### Preparation checklist

On first entry to Step 4, the charity is shown a preparation checklist covering:

- Financial documents needed (annual accounts, management accounts, budget)
- Any other supporting documents flagged by the AI from the guidelines

This is shown once per application and requires explicit dismissal.

#### Senior review prompt

Before assembling, the charity is prompted to involve a senior colleague (CEO, treasurer, or trustee) to review financial figures and confirm the application accurately represents the organisation. In the live product this is the "Before we put it together" screen, confirmed via the **Yes — assemble my draft** button.

### 5.6 Step 5 — Review and Export

The charity reviews the assembled draft, then ticks three confirmation checkboxes ("I have reviewed all responses...", "The information provided is accurate and complete...", "I understand this application was prepared with AI assistance..."). The download buttons are disabled until all three are ticked.

**There is no separate approval step.** Clicking **Download as Word document (.docx)** both approves the application and downloads it in a single action — the previous design (a separate Approve button and confirmation modal) was removed as redundant friction, since ticking three checkboxes already demonstrates intent. After the first download, a persistent "Application approved" banner replaces the checklist, and the application status changes to `exported`.

**Re-export:** clicking either download button again (Word or plain text) after the application has already been exported shows a warning dialog stating the prior export's timestamp, since the charity may already have submitted that version to the funder. The charity can cancel or proceed.

**Export formats:**

- **Word document (.docx)** — formatted, labelled, with disclaimer, attribution, and a "Page N of NN" footer line
- **Plain text (.txt)** — for copying into portals; same content and footer, no page numbers

Grant Pathway does not offer per-question copy buttons or any other mechanism for filling individual portal fields — output is always the two whole-document downloads above. This is a permanent boundary (R9), not a missing feature — see `docs/v1-out-of-scope.md`.

## 6. Limit Handling

### 6.1 Data model

`application_answers` table carries:

- `word_limit` — integer, null if not word-limited
- `char_limit` — integer, null if not character-limited
- `limit_type` — `words | characters | none`, determining which of the two limit columns is relevant for a given question

### 6.2 AI extraction

The AI prompt extracts limit type and value from guidelines text. Common patterns:

- "no more than 800 characters" → `char_limit: 800, limit_type: characters`
- "maximum 200 words" → `word_limit: 200, limit_type: words`
- "up to 400 words" → `word_limit: 400, limit_type: words`
- No limit stated → `word_limit: null, char_limit: null, limit_type: none`

### 6.3 Over-limit behaviour

Exceeding a limit triggers a visible warning (counter turns red). For narrative questions, this includes the message "Please trim it or use AI to bring it within the limit before approving" — see `PDR-AI-007` for budget/financial questions, which have no AI assist and a different message. This is a **hard stop, not advisory**: the **Approve this answer** button is hidden while any answer is over its limit — the charity cannot approve, and therefore cannot proceed to assembly, until every answer is within limit. The original "warn but allow" behaviour was removed because grant portal systems uniformly reject over-limit submissions, so allowing approval in Grant Pathway would have given false confidence.

AI assist remains available on over-limit narrative answers specifically to help bring them within the limit — see the known limitation on AI compression reliability in Section 5.5. Budget/financial answers have no AI assist at all, so this route doesn't apply to them — the charity must trim the figures manually; see `PDR-AI-007` for the budget-specific over-limit message.

## 8. Out of Scope

The following are explicitly out of scope for Grant Pathway v1 and should not be built, implied, or promised:

| Out of scope                        | Reason                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Direct portal submission            | Each funder's portal requires separate authentication and integration; not feasible at scale                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Funder discovery / grant-finding    | Out of scope for Grant Pathway itself; well-served by existing tools (GrantFinder, GRANTIS) in the interim. A dedicated grant-discovery capability (e.g. searching a grant repository for a specific need, such as solar panel funding) is being scoped as a **separate, chargeable service**, not a Grant Pathway feature — work on this is starting shortly. |
| Comprehensive eligibility screening | Grant Pathway does not confirm whether a charity is eligible; the charity must read the guidelines. `FR-47`'s mismatch hard stop only catches clear, obvious cases (e.g. a Milton-Keynes-only funder and an applicant charity based in Surrey) — it is not a substitute for the charity's own eligibility check.                                               |
| Multi-user / team collaboration     | v1 is single-user per account; team features deferred                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

**BD-02 note (2026-07-11, revised 2026-07-15):** The original "thick profile" (identity, address/contact, full financial detail, contact details) was never fully built. During the Section 4.2 review, the smaller profile actually in production — Identity, Mission and work, and (at the time) Financial and governance (10 fields total, the last five added for a concrete reason, R13) — was confirmed as the intended design going forward, not a partial build. Address/contact, most financial fields, and supporting-document status were retired as unneeded, not deferred. **The Financial and governance fields were themselves re-sited out of the profile entirely on 2026-07-15** (§4.2.3) — the charity profile is now just Identity and Mission and work (5 fields), with the governance/reserves facts collected fresh per application on Step 4 instead.

**BD-03 note (2026-07-05):** Never implemented as originally specified — the AI extraction prompt discards non-narrative questions entirely rather than extracting them for pre-fill or reminder display (surfaced during Clothworkers testing, `IT-CW-08`). A structured review of nine funders (`docs/BRD plus decisions Mark Two/question-coverage-analysis.md`) found the underlying problem was twenty distinct ways the flat, narrative-only data model doesn't match real funder requirements (R1–R20, `docs/BRD plus decisions Mark Two/clean-slate-design-proposal.md`), not simply "pre-fill or remind." **ADR-DATA-006** decides a typed item-graph model that supersedes BD-03 rather than resolving it narrowly (the model's originally-proposed "curated funder playbook" build mechanism was itself superseded before build — see ADR-DATA-006's 2026-07-14 amendment; the typed item-graph classification stands unchanged). Not yet built — see `docs/BRD plus decisions Mark Two/build-plan-any-guideline-or-form.md` for phased sequencing; Section 5.5 should be revisited once that build lands, not before.

**BD-04 note (2026-07-05):** The question-level typing mechanism itself was only partially built (see Section 5.5) — only `narrative` extraction exists in practice. Separately, "funder type (structured/narrative)" as referenced in this decision's own rationale turned out to be a _different, later-introduced_ concept than intended: it was decided in `DR-FD-001` (2026-06-01, two days after this BRD's design session) purely as a database convenience, never actually defined, and it collided in name with the unrelated, well-defined _question-level_ `narrative` type from this same BD-04. Reviewing the real guideline documents in `docs/Grant Org Guidelines/` found it didn't reflect a stable property of any funder either — several funders (Henry Smith, Idlewild) produce both a discrete-question form and free-form guidance, depending on which document is uploaded. This is what `DR-FD-001` (v1.0 → v1.2) retires — the funder-level "Structured"/"Narrative" picker badge, found not to reflect a stable property of any funder.

**Resolved 2026-07-05:** BD-04's original instinct — question-level typing, not funder-level typing, as the primary classification — was correct, and is exactly what **ADR-DATA-006**'s item-graph model fully realises: every item (narrative, data, date, number, table, file, consent, eligibility gate, scoring criterion, manual action) is typed and classified individually. BD-04 does not need further direct amendment; it is subsumed into ADR-DATA-006 going forward.

**BD-07 note (2026-07-11):** Withdrawn. The tier display was never built, and the premise it relied on — coverage level as a stable per-funder property — was disproven by the same finding that led to retiring the funder-level "Structured"/"Narrative" picker badge (`DR-FD-001` v1.0 → v1.2): the same funder can behave differently depending on which guidelines document is uploaded. Charities are no worse off, since no such display has ever existed. See `moscow-feature-register.md` FR-46 and `docs/v1-out-of-scope.md`.

**BD-06 note (2026-07-11):** The stated rationale ("simplicity preferred") overstates the technical cost of linking applications — `applications` already carries both `user_id` and `funder_id`, so querying a charity's application history with a specific funder requires no schema change, just a query; this was never actually the hard part. The real reason a scored, no-question criterion like R16 (MK Community Foundation's Group Profile Score) can't be answered isn't a linkage-engineering problem at all: Grant Pathway has no visibility into funder-side outcome or relationship data (won/lost, prior funding, relationship depth) regardless of whether our own application records are linked — it never observes a funder's decision. Linking Grant Pathway's own records to each other would not solve R16; the barrier is data access, not engineering effort.

Confirmed instead: where Step 3 (AI summary) or the item-graph (Step 4, once built — `ADR-DATA-006`) identifies a scored criterion with no answerable question — the R16 pattern — Grant Pathway will disclose it explicitly to the charity as a visible, non-fillable item ("this factor affects your score with this funder and is outside what Grant Pathway can assess — you'll need to address it in the form or with the funder directly"), rather than silently omitting it. This is the same disclosure principle already decided for R17 (`clean-slate-design-proposal.md` — disclose, don't attempt).

## 11. Help Centre and Contextual Guidance

Grant Pathway provides two forms of in-app guidance, added 2026-07-24 (`PDR-UI-008`):

**A persistent link to an external help centre**, present in the nav bar (both signed-in and signed-out), the global footer, and the dashboard's empty state for a user with no applications yet. Every instance opens the help centre in a new browser tab, so the charity never loses an in-progress form or application.

**Contextual tooltips at known points of friction** — 10 short, plain-language hints attached to specific fields or buttons across the charity profile, application steps, and account settings (e.g. explaining the Charity Commission lookup, why a budget question has no AI assist, or what's still needed before a draft can be assembled). Each tooltip appears on mouse hover or keyboard focus and disappears when the user moves away — there is no dismiss control and no memory of having been shown before; a tooltip shows exactly the same way every time, for every user, on every visit. An earlier design persisted a per-user "dismissed" state server-side; this was built, then reversed the same week (2026-07-25) as disproportionate complexity for a pre-launch product with no real users yet to have earned it.

See `PDR-UI-008` for the full per-tooltip specification and `docs/Test Plans/help-and-tooltips-test-plan.md` for live-testing results (`FR-49`, `moscow-feature-register.md`).

**Reviewed 2026-08-05 (`P5.0`, register ref R-07) — accurate.** Verified against `components/contextual-tooltip.tsx` and its ten call sites, `PDR-UI-008` v3.0 and the 2026-07-25 persistence reversal. The "appears on mouse hover **or keyboard focus**" claim above is true, but only became true on 2026-07-25: `GAP-38` found three tooltips (`tt-budget-no-ai`, `tt-guidelines-choice`, `tt-summary-review`) wrapping plain `div`/`p`/`h1` elements with no `tabIndex`, so keyboard users could never reach them — a real shipped WCAG 2.1.1 failure, found by the single keyboard step in HT-05 and fixed the same session with an explicit `tabIndex={0}` and focus-visible ring. **The count of ten is correct here**; note that "nine" appears in several test and audit documents and is being reconciled under register ref **R-08**.
