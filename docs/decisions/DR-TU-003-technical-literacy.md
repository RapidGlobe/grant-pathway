# DR-TU-003 — Expected Technical Literacy of Users

## Question

What level of technical literacy should the app be designed for?

## Context

The assumed technical literacy of users drives decisions about UI complexity, onboarding, help content, and accessibility. Many small charity workers are not technically confident and may be intimidated by unfamiliar software or AI tools. If the app assumes high technical literacy, it can move faster and offer more power features. If it targets low technical literacy, it needs simpler flows, more guidance, and plain language throughout. This also affects how AI interactions are surfaced — whether users prompt the AI directly or interact through guided forms.

## Options

- **Option A: Low** — Designed for users with minimal digital confidence; plain language, guided workflows, no assumed familiarity with AI tools
- **Option B: Medium** — Assumes basic digital literacy (email, web forms, Google Docs) but not specialist software knowledge
- **Option C: High** — Assumes comfort with SaaS tools and possibly AI assistants; power features and efficiency prioritised over hand-holding

## Decision

**Option B: Medium** — The app assumes basic digital literacy (email, web forms, Google Docs) but not familiarity with specialist software or AI tools.

## Rationale

Medium literacy is the realistic baseline for most charity workers today, including volunteers. The app should never require familiarity with AI — AI works quietly in the background, surfaced through simple guided forms rather than open-ended prompting. This keeps the app accessible to the primary target user (DR-TU-001) without artificially capping the product's capability or creating an overly hand-held experience that would frustrate more confident users.
