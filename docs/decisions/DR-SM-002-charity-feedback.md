# DR-SM-002 — How Charities Will Provide Feedback

## Question

How will charities provide feedback on the app?

## Context

Charity feedback is essential for improving the app and demonstrating impact to funders or donors. Feedback mechanisms must be lightweight enough that time-poor charity workers actually use them. Options range from passive (in-app ratings) to active (interviews and user research). A feedback channel also builds a relationship with the charity community using the tool. The method chosen should be sustainable — a feedback approach that requires significant manual effort to manage may not last.

## Options

- **Option A: In-app rating/thumbs** — Simple thumbs up/down or star rating on AI outputs and features; minimal friction
- **Option B: Periodic email survey** — Send a short survey (e.g. 3-5 questions) to active users quarterly
- **Option C: Open feedback form** — A always-accessible form for charities to submit suggestions and bug reports
- **Option D: User interviews** — Proactively recruit charities for periodic one-to-one feedback calls
- **Option E: Community forum** — A shared space (e.g. Slack group, forum) where charities discuss the app and suggest improvements
- **Option F: Combination** — In-app ratings for passive feedback plus a periodic survey for structured feedback

## Decision

**Option D: User interviews** — Feedback will be gathered through proactive one-to-one conversations with charities. Contact details (email) are collected at registration, and willingness to participate in feedback calls is captured via an explicit opt-in checkbox at registration. This approach will be revisited and expanded (e.g. adding an open feedback form or in-app ratings) once a meaningful user base is established.

## Rationale

At early-stage user volumes, one-to-one interviews provide the richest and most actionable feedback. The contact detail problem is solved through the registration flow — email is collected as a functional necessity, and a simple opt-in at registration identifies charities willing to participate. This is fully GDPR-compliant. More scalable feedback mechanisms (surveys, in-app ratings, community forums) are better suited to a later phase when user volumes make them meaningful.
