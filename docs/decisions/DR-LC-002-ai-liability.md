# DR-LC-002 — Liability for AI-Generated Application Content

## Question

What is the liability position if AI-generated content in the app leads to a failed, incorrect, or misleading grant application?

## Context

AI language models can produce inaccurate, misleading, or inappropriate content. If a charity uses AI-generated text in a grant application and that content contains errors or misrepresents the charity, it could result in application rejection, reputational damage with funders, or in extreme cases allegations of misrepresentation. The app's Terms of Service must address this, but the design of the app should also reflect the liability position — for example, by requiring human review (DR-AI-003) or displaying prominent disclaimers. Legal advice should be sought to define this position clearly.

## Options

- **Option A: No liability — user responsibility** — Terms of Service make clear that all AI-generated content is the user's responsibility to verify; the app accepts no liability
- **Option B: Limited liability with disclaimers** — Clear in-app disclaimers that AI content must be reviewed; liability limited to the maximum extent permitted by law
- **Option C: Liability insurance** — The app operator takes out professional indemnity or product liability insurance to cover potential claims
- **Option D: Seek legal advice before launch** — Commission specific legal advice on liability exposure and draft Terms of Service accordingly before any charity uses the app

## Decision

**Option A: No liability — user responsibility** — The app is a writing aid only. The user is responsible for all content they submit to funders. The Terms of Service will make this position unambiguous in plain language.

## Rationale

The worst-case scenario is a rejected grant application — a bad outcome but not a catastrophic one, and one that occurs constantly without any app being involved. The app makes no promises about funding outcomes, and the mandatory human review step (DR-AI-003) means the charity has explicitly confirmed all content before using it. The user, not the app, is accountable for what is submitted to funders. This position is entirely defensible provided the Terms of Service articulates it clearly.

## Terms of Service Requirement

The following must be stated clearly and prominently in the Terms of Service — in plain language, not legal boilerplate:

**What this app does not do:**

- Guarantee or promise funding
- Submit applications on behalf of charities
- Make any representations to funders directly
