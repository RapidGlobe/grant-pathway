# DR-OD-003 — Donation Recipient: One Charity or the Whole Sector

## Question

Will the app be donated to one specific charity, or made available to the charity sector as a whole?

## Context

Donating to a single charity gives a clear custodian and simplifies governance, but limits the app's reach and may create a perception that it serves that charity's interests rather than the sector. Making it available to the whole sector maximises impact but requires a neutral stewardship structure and raises questions about who is responsible for maintenance, costs, and strategic direction. This decision is closely linked to DR-OD-001 (ownership) but focuses specifically on access breadth.

## Options

- **Option A: One specific charity** — Identify and partner with a single charity (e.g. NCVO, a local charity hub) to receive and steward the app
- **Option B: The sector broadly** — The app is available to any registered UK charity with no restriction
- **Option C: A charity infrastructure body** — Donate to a sector-support organisation (e.g. NCVO, ACEVO, Locality) that serves many charities

## Decision

**Option B: The sector broadly** — Any registered UK charity can access and use the app. There is no single recipient charity; the tool belongs to the sector as a whole.

## Rationale

Open access to all registered UK charities is fully consistent with the umbrella CIC model (DR-OD-001) and the completely free access decision (DR-OD-002). Neutral, sector-wide ownership avoids any perception of bias. Sector infrastructure bodies such as NCVO, Catalyst, and Locality are better engaged as distribution and promotion partners rather than custodians — giving the app reach and credibility without concentrating control in any one organisation.
