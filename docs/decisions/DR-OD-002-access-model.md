# DR-OD-002 — Charity Access and Pricing Model

## Question

How will charities access the app — and will they pay for it?

## Context

The access model determines sustainability, adoption barriers, and perceived value. A fully free tool removes the cost barrier but requires external funding to cover infrastructure and AI API costs. A freemium model allows basic access for free while charging for advanced features. A subscription model creates recurring revenue but may exclude the smallest charities with the least budget. Since the app is intended as a donation to charities, a free or subsidised model may be preferred — but ongoing costs (AI API calls, hosting) must be covered somehow.

## Options

- **Option A: Completely free** — No cost to any charity; costs absorbed by the owner/operator or covered by grants/donations to the app itself
- **Option B: Freemium** — Core features free; advanced features (e.g. unlimited applications, team accounts) require a paid tier
- **Option C: Subsidised subscription** — A low-cost subscription (e.g. £5–20/month) to cover costs, with hardship waivers available
- **Option D: Free for small charities, paid for larger** — Tiered by charity size or income
- **Option E: Grant-funded operational model** — The app operator applies for grants to fund free access for charities

## Decision

**Option A: Completely free** — The app is free to all charities with no cost, no freemium tier, and no subscription.

## Rationale

Completely free removes all adoption barriers and is fully consistent with the donation ethos. At early usage volumes, AI API and hosting costs are manageable. Once the CIC is established (see DR-OD-001), the intention is to pursue operational grant funding from digital/tech-for-good funders (e.g. Nominet, Catalyst, Comic Relief Tech for Good) to cover infrastructure costs at scale — transitioning from personally funded to grant-funded operations without ever charging charities.
