# DR-OD-001 — Long-Term Ownership and Operation

## Question

Who will own and operate the app long-term?

## Context

The ownership structure affects governance, funding, accountability, and how decisions about the app's future are made. Options range from individual ownership (you personally) to a formal charitable or social enterprise structure. If the app is truly donated to the sector, a stewardship model is needed so it can continue to function without depending on any one individual. This decision also has legal and tax implications.

## Options

- **Option A: Individual ownership** — You own and operate the app personally in perpetuity
- **Option B: Community Interest Company (CIC)** — A CIC is established to own and run the app with a community benefit lock
- **Option C: Donated to an existing charity** — Ownership transferred to a specific UK charity who takes on stewardship
- **Option D: Open source community** — The app is released as open source and governed by a contributor community
- **Option E: Charitable trust or foundation** — A new charitable entity is created to own the app on behalf of the sector

## Decision

**Option A now, transitioning to Option B (CIC) post-launch** — Individual ownership during build and early launch. A CIC will be established once the app is proven and has active users.

## Rationale

Individual ownership is pragmatic for the build phase and avoids governance overhead that would delay launch. The long-term destination is a CIC, which provides a community benefit lock, institutional credibility in the charity sector, and a governance structure that doesn't depend on one individual. When the CIC is established, it should be scoped as an **umbrella entity** — not app-specific — as the intention is to build and donate multiple digital tools to the UK charity sector over time. This avoids needing to create separate legal entities for future apps.
